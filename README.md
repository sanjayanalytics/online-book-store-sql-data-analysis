# Online Book Store Data Analysis Using SQL

## Project Overview
This project analyzes an Online Book Store database using PostgreSQL to explore book inventory, sales, revenue, and customer purchasing behavior. I used basic and advanced SQL queries to answer business questions and extract meaningful insights from the data.

## Project Objectives
- Analyze book inventory, genres, and pricing.
- Evaluate sales performance and revenue.
- Identify popular books and genres.
- Understand customer purchasing behavior.
- Analyze remaining stock after fulfilling orders.

## Tools & Technologies
- PostgreSQL
- SQL
- pgAdmin

## Database Tables
- Books
- Customers
- Orders

## Basic SQL Queries
1. Retrieve all books in the Fiction genre.
2. Find books published after 1950.
3. List customers from Canada.
4. Show orders placed in November 2023.
5. Retrieve the total stock of available books.
6. Find the most expensive book.
7. Identify orders with a quantity greater than one.
8. Retrieve customers who placed more than one order.
9. Find orders with a total amount exceeding $20.
10. List all genres available in the Books table.
11. Find books with the lowest stock.
12. Calculate total revenue generated from all orders.
13. Retrieve November 2023 orders with book titles.

## Advanced SQL Queries
1. Calculate the total quantity of books sold for each genre.
2. Find the average price of books in the Fantasy genre.
3. Identify customers who placed at least two orders.
4. Find the most frequently ordered book.
5. Retrieve the top three most expensive Fantasy books.
6. Calculate the total quantity of books sold by each author.
7. Identify cities where customers who spent over $30 are located.
8. Find the highest-spending customer.
9. Calculate the remaining stock after fulfilling orders.

## Key Findings and Learnings
1. **Sales and Revenue Analysis:** Total revenue generated was $75,628.66.
2. **Customer Spending:** Kim Turner (Customer ID: 457) was the highest-spending customer.
3. **Customer Purchasing Behavior:** Identified 139 customers who placed multiple orders and analyzed their spending.
4. **Inventory Management:** Analyzed total inventory and remaining stock after fulfilling orders.
5. **Practical SQL Skills:** Improved my understanding of filtering, sorting, aggregate functions, JOINs, GROUP BY, HAVING, subqueries, and COALESCE.

## Project Conclusion
This project strengthened my practical SQL skills and demonstrated how PostgreSQL can be used to analyze bookstore data, answer business questions, and generate insights to support data-driven decisions.
